UW admissions guidance

EasyDawg's evaluation logic is grounded in the University of Washington's published guidance on the personal statement and its holistic review. The two frameworks below are the backbone of how the Evaluator Engine reads an essay.

The Answer–Evidence–Interpretation framework

UW advises students to build every essay around three moves. A strong response does all three; a weak one usually skips the third.

  1. Answer. State directly what the prompt is actually asking. The reader should know, within the first lines, the specific experience, belief, or moment the essay is about — no throat-clearing or generic preamble.
  2. Evidence. Ground the answer in concrete, specific detail: the scene, the dialogue, the exact thing you did. Evidence is what separates a story only you could tell from an essay a thousand students could have submitted.
  3. Interpretation. Explain what the evidence means — how it changed your thinking, what it reveals about you, why it matters. This is the reflective move that turns a recounted event into insight, and it is the single biggest differentiator between an average essay and a memorable one.

The six scoring dimensions map onto this framework: focus and specificity reward a clear answer backed by real evidence, while how you think, character, and self-awareness reward genuine interpretation.

Holistic review criteria

UW reads each application as a whole rather than against a single cutoff. The essay is weighed against criteria like these:

  • Intellectual curiosity & how you think. Evidence of a mind that questions, reframes, and reflects — not just what you concluded, but how you got there.
  • Personal character & growth. Honesty, resilience, and a demonstrated arc of change over time, including how you responded to setbacks.
  • Contribution to community. The ways you have added to the people and places around you, and the perspective you would bring to campus.
  • Context. Your accomplishments are read against the opportunities and obstacles you actually had, not in a vacuum.
  • Self-awareness. A clear-eyed understanding of yourself — your values, your limits, and what you have learned.

Holistic review means no single number decides an application. The essay is your chance to supply the interpretation the rest of the file cannot.
